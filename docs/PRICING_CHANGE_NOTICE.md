# Pricing change — minimum order value

**Effective:** when Stage 1 ships.
**Who needs to read this:** anyone who quotes a price to a customer.

## What changed

The Minimum Order Value (₦1,000 by default) used to be charged **once for every
line** on an order. It is now charged **once for the whole order**.

## What it means at the counter

| Order | Old price | New price |
|---|---|---|
| One ₦600 job | ₦1,000 | ₦1,000 — **no change** |
| Two ₦600 jobs | ₦2,000 | ₦1,200 |
| Three ₦600 jobs | ₦3,000 | ₦1,800 |
| Anything already above ₦1,000 in total | unchanged | unchanged |

**Single-item orders do not change. Nothing above the minimum changes.**
The only orders affected are multi-item orders whose items are individually
small — those get cheaper.

## Why

"Minimum Order Value" means a minimum on the order. Charging it per line meant a
customer bringing three small jobs paid the minimum three times, which was never
the intent. It was a bug, not a policy.

## What you need to do

- **If you have quoted a multi-item small order verbally and not yet entered it,
  re-check the price in the app before invoicing.** The app will now say a lower
  number than you may have quoted.
- **Saved quotes are honoured at their original price.** Converting an existing
  quote to a sale carries its stored total across unchanged — it is not
  re-priced. You do not need to re-issue anything.
- If a customer queries an older invoice, the old price was correct at the time.
  This changes new orders only; nothing historic has been rewritten.

## Also visible

The minimum now appears on the invoice as its own line — **"Minimum order
adjustment: +₦400"** — instead of being folded silently into the subtotal.
Delivery appears as its own line too. The subtotal is now genuinely the sum of
the printing, so the invoice adds up in front of the customer.

Delivery still does **not** count toward reaching the minimum: a ₦600 job with
₦2,000 delivery is still ₦3,000, not ₦2,600. That is deliberate and unchanged.
