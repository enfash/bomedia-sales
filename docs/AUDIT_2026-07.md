## How the ratchet works

Each bug is bracketed by a **pair** of tests. Both pass today and **both go red
the moment the bug is fixed**, so neither the fix nor the test can be forgotten:

| | Test | Asserts | Today | On fix | Stage 1 action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | `it.failing(…)` | the **correct** behaviour | passes | fails | flip `it.failing` → `it` |
| **B** | `it('current behaviour: …')` | today's **wrong** value | passes | fails | **delete** |

Test A never encodes the wrong output, so flipping it is a one-word diff.
Test B exists because `it.failing` only checks *that* the body throws, not why —
a broken import or bad fixture would make A pass while proving nothing. B runs
the same import and fixture through a plain assertion, so any such breakage
surfaces as an ordinary failure.

Some tests in each block are marked *permanent* below: they pass now, stay
passing after the fix, and must **not** be deleted.

> **Invariant: when Stage 1 is complete, no `it.failing` remains in the suite.**
> `grep -rn "it.failing(" src/` must return nothing.

## Item 2 · Bug 1 — "Today" is computed in UTC

`computeDashboardMetrics`

**Fix direction:** standardise on local components (or an explicit

## Item 4 · Bug 3 — Float error renders fractional kobo

**§1.3 #3-adjacent** · [`src/utils/currency.ts:9`](../src/utils/currency.ts#L9) ·
`formatCurrency`

A bare `toLocaleString()` defaults to `maximumFractionDigits: 3`, so an amount
carrying accumulated float error renders as `₦1,234.56` on a real invoice.

Tests — `src/utils/__tests__/currency.test.ts`, block
`formatCurrency › §1.3 bug #3 — fractional kobo from float error`

| | Test name | Action |
|---|---|---|
| A | `renders a float-drifted amount as whole naira` | flip → `it` |
| B | `current behaviour: renders a float-drifted amount with stray decimals` | delete |
| — | `the drifted value is within a kobo of 1234.56` | **permanent** |

**Decided:** whole-naira rounding app-wide. `formatCurrency(1234.56)` also
renders `₦1,235`. The existing tests for `0`, `600`, `1000`, `1_000_000` and
`-2500` must keep passing.

### The money policy this fix belongs to

Formatting is the *last* line of defence, not the fix. The rounding rule is:

1. **Each line total is rounded to whole naira at write time.**
2. **`subtotal` = sum of the rounded line totals.
   `totalAmount` = `subtotal` + rounded adjustments.**
3. **Neither `subtotal` nor `totalAmount` is ever rounded independently.**
4. **Payment amounts are rounded to whole naira at write, using the same
   `money.ts` helpers as line totals.**

With that in place `formatCurrency`'s rounding becomes a no-op safety net and
the invoice always adds up — the line items a customer reads sum to the
subtotal, and every naira above it is a named adjustment row.

#### Why rule 4 exists

`totalPaid` becomes a sum over payment children. Unrounded payment amounts
drift, so `totalBalance` never reaches exactly zero on a fully-paid invoice —
and that **breaks the Paid status derivation**, because
[`computePaymentStatus`](../src/utils/payment-status.ts#L51) tests
`totalPaid >= totalAmount`. A few kobo of accumulated float is the difference
between an invoice reading **Paid** and reading **Partial** forever.

#### Rule 4 is a precondition on Stage 2, not a gate on Stage 1

Stage 1 does not touch payments. Rule 4 constrains **Stage 2** (append-only
payment children, `totalPaid` written via transaction/`increment`) and blocks
no Stage 1 item. Two requirements land on Stage 2:

- **Adopt the rule from `money.ts`, do not restate it.** Stage 2 imports the
  same rounding helpers line totals use. A second copy of the rule is a second
  thing to keep in sync.
- **Round the migration's synthetic opening entries.** The migration folds each
  existing `totalPaid` into one synthetic opening payment. Written unrounded,
  the drift is baked into permanent history at migration time — and no later
  fix recovers it, because the original unrounded inputs are gone.

> Rule 2 is the **amended** form. The earlier draft said "the batch total is the
> sum of the line totals", which is only true when no adjustment applies. The
> line sum is the **subtotal**; `totalAmount` legitimately exceeds it once MOV
> or delivery are added. Tests must assert against `subtotal`, never
> `totalAmount` — hence the amend-then-flip actions below.

Tests — `src/services/__tests__/sales-repository.test.ts`, block
`normalizeBatch › money invariant — rounded line totals sum to the batch total`

Fixture: ₦150/sqft × 3.75 sqft = **₦562.5** per line, three lines. Today that
stores lines of `562.5` and a batch of `1687.5`; rounding the lines afterwards

### What "amend-then-flip" means

`subtotal` and `adjustments[]` **do not exist yet** on `SalesBatch`. The tests
reference them through a `BatchWithMoneyFields` widening declared at the top of
`sales-repository.test.ts`, so `tsc --noEmit` stays green while the ratchets
fail on `undefined` — the correct signal.

An amend-then-flip test therefore needs **two** edits, in order:

1. **Amend** — add the real fields to the types, then delete the
   `BatchWithMoneyFields` / `withMoneyFields` / `storedWithMoneyFields` helpers
   and read the fields directly.
2. **Flip** — `it.failing` → `it`.

> ⚠️ **Do not flip these mechanically.** Converting them while they still read
> `totalAmount` would pin the wrong field: `totalAmount` is *supposed* to exceed
> the line sum once an adjustment applies. The line sum is the `subtotal`.

### Business rule to preserve — MOV is a minimum on printing, not on the invoice

Today's expression is `Math.max(batchSubtotal, mov) + delivery`, where
`batchSubtotal` is **goods only**. The `+ delivery` sitting *outside* the
`max()` is what confines MOV to printing. **This behaviour is correct and must
be kept.**

Worked case — ₦600 of printing, ₦2,000 delivery, ₦1,000 MOV:

| Reading | Calculation | Charged | MOV top-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Goods-only (correct)** | `max(600, 1000) + 2000` | **₦3,000** | ₦400 |
| Invoice-wide (wrong) | `max(600 + 2000, 1000)` | ₦2,600 | none |

Delivery is pass-through, not printing revenue — a large delivery must not let a
tiny print job dodge the minimum. Today this is an accident of operator
precedence; in `money.ts` it must be an **explicit, commented decision** that a
later refactor cannot silently invert.

Tests — `src/services/__tests__/sales-repository.test.ts`, block
`normalizeBatch › MOV applies to printing only, not to the invoice total`

| | Test name | Action |
|---|---|---|
| — | `carries the ₦3,000 charged on a ₦600 job with ₦2,000 delivery` | **permanent** |
| — | `specifies goods-only (₦3,000) over invoice-wide (₦2,600)` | **permanent** |
| A | `records the MOV top-up and delivery as separate adjustments` | **amend-then-flip** |
| B | `current behaviour: the ₦400 top-up is invisible in the stored batch` | delete |

> ⚠️ These two permanent tests **document** the rule; they do not guard it. The
> fixture hard-codes `totalAmount` because no pure function computes it yet.
>
> **Stage 1 owes a `money.ts` test that asserts the ₦3,000 is computed, not
> read from a fixture** — i.e. it calls the money.ts total function with
> `goods = 600`, `delivery = 2000`, `mov = 1000` as *inputs* and asserts the
> returned value is `3000`. A test that reads `3000` from a fixture, as the two
> above do, does not discharge this requirement.

### `adjustments[]` is an immutable write-time snapshot

Adjustment amounts are priced **once, at write time**, and stored on the batch.
They are **never** recomputed from live Settings on read. Otherwise raising the
MOV next quarter would silently restate every historic invoice, and a reprinted
receipt would not match the one the customer already paid against.

`normalizeBatch` takes no settings argument, so the guarantee currently holds
structurally. The tests pin it behaviourally so a future "helpful" refactor
cannot break it.

Tests — block
`normalizeBatch › adjustments are a write-time snapshot, never recomputed on read`

| | Test name | Action |
|---|---|---|
| A | `preserves a historic ₦500 MOV top-up under a current ₦1,000 MOV` | **amend-then-flip** |
| — | `returns the same result for one node under two different MOV settings` | **permanent** |

The permanent test normalizes one fixture twice under `mockMov = 500` then
`1_000` and compares. It is a **tripwire**: `normalizeBatch` imports nothing
settings-related today, so the mock of `@/context/settings-context` is inert. It
starts biting the moment someone makes the normalizer read the live MOV.

> ⚠️ **Review this block when item 3 lands.** Item 3 makes the overdue threshold
> a parameter of `normalizeBatch`. The tripwire's claim is "same stored node →
> same result"; once an input is added, that must be restated as "same node
> **and same threshold** → same result" and both calls must pass the same
> threshold. Left alone it would still pass, but for the wrong reason.

## ⚠️ Offline writes are lost silently — a dependency problem, not a polish item

Found 2026-08-01 while planning Stage 2. **This is not a Stage 5 UX task.** It is
a data-loss path in the tool's primary use case, and fixing it properly means
changing a dependency.

### What happens today

`src/lib/firebase.ts` uses the **JavaScript `firebase` SDK (v12)**:

```ts
import { getDatabase } from 'firebase/database';
```

| | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Local echo of writes | **yes** — applied to the in-memory cache immediately, listeners fire at once |
| `ServerValue.increment` offline | **yes** — resolved locally as an estimate, re-applied atomically on reconnect |
| **On-disk write queue** | **NO** |

`setPersistenceEnabled` / `setPersistenceCacheSizeBytes` do not exist in the JS
SDK — they are Android/iOS native-SDK features. The JS SDK's outstanding-write
queue lives in memory only.

### The failure

An operator at the counter, offline:

1. Records a payment. The write queues in memory.
2. Listeners fire from the local cache — **the balance drops, the payment
   appears in the history, the invoice reads Paid.** It looks completely
   successful, because from the app's point of view it succeeded.
3. No pending-sync indicator exists, so there is nothing to suggest otherwise.
4. The app is force-quit, crashes, or is reclaimed by the OS before it
   reconnects.
5. **The payment is gone.** No error, no trace, no way to know it happened.

The operator watched the money go in. The database never heard about it.

This is worse for a payment than for a sale: an un-synced sale is usually
noticed because the job still has to be printed, but an un-synced payment leaves
a customer marked unpaid who has already paid, and a drawer that does not
reconcile with no record of why.

### Secondary effect on the write path

`await update(...)` does not resolve until the server acks, so offline the
`await` hangs. The modal never closes and no feedback appears, so the operator
taps again. Under append-only payments that queues **two ledger entries** rather
than one wrong number.

Mitigated in Stage 2 by a submit guard plus closing on the local echo rather
than the ack — but that only stops the duplicate, not the loss.

### The real fix — plan for it

Swap the JS SDK for **`@react-native-firebase/database`**, which has genuine
on-disk persistence and a durable write queue:

```js
firebase.database().setPersistenceEnabled(true);
```

This is a dependency change with real scope, not a patch:

- `@react-native-firebase/app` + `/database` + `/auth` must be added together;
  they cannot be mixed with the JS SDK for the same services
- it requires native modules — **no longer Expo Go**; needs a development build
  or prebuild, which changes how the app is run and tested day to day
- `src/lib/firebase.ts`, `src/services/db.ts` and `src/context/auth-context.tsx`
  all rebind to the new client
- the **web build cannot use it** — web keeps the JS SDK, so `db.ts` gains a
  platform split, and the `.web.tsx` twins already in the codebase become a
  pattern that extends to the data layer

### Interim, until that lands

- treat any offline-recorded payment as unconfirmed until seen on a second
  device
- do not force-quit the app after taking payment offline
- Stage 5's connection indicator and pending-sync badge become **the visible
  warning that this failure mode exists**, which is worth more than the polish
  they were scheduled as

## A void is held by TWO rules, not one

Recorded 2026-08-01. **Read this before relaxing any delete rule.**

Void permanence depends on two independent rules. Remove either and voids stop
being permanent, but only one of them looks like it is about voids.

**Rule 1 — `.validate` on the batch node.** Once `voidedAtMs` exists, no write
may change any void field:

```json
".validate": "!data.child('voidedAtMs').exists() || (
   newData.child('voidedAtMs').val()   === data.child('voidedAtMs').val() &&
   newData.child('voidedBy').val()     === data.child('voidedBy').val() &&
   newData.child('voidedByName').val() === data.child('voidedByName').val() &&
   newData.child('voidReason').val()   === data.child('voidReason').val())"
```

This pins the **values**, not merely their existence. An earlier version checked
only `newData.child('voidedAtMs').exists()`, which allowed the timestamp to be
overwritten with a fresh one — disguising when the void happened — and left
`voidedBy` and `voidReason` unprotected entirely.

**Rule 2 — `newData.exists()` on the same node's `.write`.**

> ⚠️ **`.validate` is not evaluated when the new value is `null`.** RTDB skips
> validation entirely on deletes. So `set('/sales/…/$id', null)` walks straight
> past rule 1. What stops it is the `newData.exists()` clause in the `.write`
> rule, which blocks removal for every client including admin.

**The consequence for whoever comes next:** if a cleanup script needs deletes
and someone relaxes `newData.exists()` to get it working, they will also, and
silently, unpin every void in the database. The delete rule is doing two jobs.
Neither rule is sufficient alone.

The same applies to quotes, which carry identical rules.

## Un-voiding: the escape hatch, and who may use it

There is no un-void **from the app**, by design. But the recovery path exists
and should be used rather than worked around.

**Do not re-create a mis-voided sale under a new receipt number.** If it has
already been invoiced, the customer now holds a document whose number matches
nothing, and the ledger has payments pointing at the voided original. One
audited manual repair is a better outcome than a duplicate record.

**Who:** the owner/admin only — the same person who holds the service account.

**How**, in order:

1. **Take a full export first.** `DATABASE_RUNBOOK.md` §Exports. This is a
   manual edit to a financial record; it gets the same treatment as any other.
2. Remove the void fields, either through the Firebase console's data editor or
   with a short Admin SDK script. **Both bypass security rules**, which is
   exactly why the app cannot do this and they can.
   Fields to clear: `voidedAt`, `voidedAtMs`, `voidedBy`, `voidedByName`,
   `voidReason`.
3. **Record it** — in `docs/` alongside the incident file, or in the activity
   feed by hand: which sale, who un-voided it, when, and why the void was wrong.
   An un-void that leaves no trace is worse than the void it corrects.

Re-adding `firebase-admin` and the service account key is covered in
`DATABASE_RUNBOOK.md` §"Write access at root depth".

## Why the ledger is NOT `/ledger/{saleId}/{entryId}`

Recorded 2026-08-01, because it is the obvious shape and will be re-proposed.

The ledger is `payments/{YYYY-MM-DD}/{uid}/{key}`. Keying it by sale instead
would make "this sale's payments" a direct read — genuinely simpler for the
transaction screen. It was rejected because it breaks two things that are
load-bearing, and neither is recoverable by adding an index:

**1. The day bucket is what makes Daily Cash a single read.** Reconciliation
asks "what was taken on this date", and the answer is one node. Keyed by sale,
the same question becomes a scan across every sale that has ever existed,
growing forever. The cash view is the reason the ledger exists; making its
primary query the expensive one inverts the design.

Note the bucket is keyed by **payment** date, not sale date — a payment taken
today against last month's invoice belongs in today's drawer.

**2. The uid level is the only place a per-staff `.read` can live.** RTDB
`.read` is all-or-nothing per node: reading `payments/{day}` returns everything
beneath it. "Staff read only their own entries" is therefore inexpressible
unless uid is a path segment. Under `/ledger/{saleId}/`, entries from several
staff share a node, and the only options are all-or-nothing — which would mean
either staff see every colleague's takings, or they see none of their own.

That would also make it the app's second instance of UI-only RBAC, which §1.4
already flags as a problem.

**What was done instead:** a pointer index colocated on the sale,
`sales/…/{saleId}/paymentRefs/{key}` -> `"{dayKey}/{uid}"`, written in the same
atomic update as the entry. The lookup becomes structural without moving the
ledger. The ref is an **index, not a record** — it says where the money is, not
that there is money — so losing one costs discoverability and is repaired by
`planPaymentRefBackfill`.

### A ref can be deleted, and the rules cannot stop it

`paymentRefs/$key` carries a create-only `.validate`
(`!data.exists() || newData.val() === data.val()`), which prevents a ref being
*changed*. It does not prevent one being *deleted*, and this could not be fixed:

- `.validate` is skipped when the written value is `null` — the same trap as
  the void pin.
- The `newData.exists()` clause on the sale's `.write` rule does **not** help
  here. When a descendant is written, that ancestor rule is evaluated with
  `newData` being the sale node *after* the write — and deleting one ref leaves
  the sale node very much in existence. So the check passes and the write is
  allowed.
- A `.write` rule on `paymentRefs/$key` itself would never be consulted: RTDB
  grants on the shallowest matching rule and does not consult deeper ones once
  an ancestor permits.

**Why this is tolerable:** the entries under `payments/` are create-only,
immutable, and unaffected. The money record is intact; only the index into it
is lost, and `scripts` can rebuild it from the ledger in one pass. That is what
makes the backfill worth having written before it is needed.

## Finding — the test harness could not run a component test at all

Discovered 2026-08-02, while writing the first one. Two independent
misconfigurations, either of which alone would have been enough.

**1. `moduleNameMapper` replaced the preset's instead of merging.** Jest does
not merge that key — declaring it in `jest.config.js` discards the preset's
entirely. `jest-expo` maps five patterns, including:

```
^react-native($|/.*)
```

Without it, `react-native` resolved to untransformed source. `@testing-library`'s
`render` then silently no-opped, returning `{}` and leaving `screen` at its
`notImplemented` placeholder — which surfaces as `` `render` function has not
been called ``, pointing at the test rather than the config.

**2. `constants/theme.ts` does `import '@/global.css'`** for Nativewind, and
Jest has no CSS transformer. Anything reaching the theme — which is almost every
component — failed to parse before it ran.

Both are fixed: the preset's mappings are spread in after the project's own, and
`.css` maps to a stub.

### What this did and did not mean

**It did not mean the suite was over-reporting.** No component test existed
before this — `src/components/**/__tests__` was created for the first time on
2026-08-02. Every test the suite counted was a real test that really ran. No
result was ever silently wrong.

**What it did mean is narrower and still serious:** an entire *category* of test
was impossible, and nothing said so. The suite's shape —
`src/services/**` and `src/utils/**`, no components — read as a deliberate
choice (Stage 0 did say "pure functions only"), when it was also a hard
constraint nobody had noticed. Anyone writing the first component test would
have hit an error naming the wrong culprit and could reasonably have concluded
the component was at fault.

The honest reading of the numbers: the 315 → 322 jump is all new ground. Of the
10 tests added with the banner, 4 are pure derivation and **6 are renders that
could not have existed the day before** — new capability, not repaired tests.

### Why it matters beyond the fix

Stage 0's premise was that the pure layer is where the money bugs live, and that
held. But it left the app's most error-prone surfaces — loading states,
conditional rendering, anything touching a subscription — with no test route at
all, and the gap was invisible from the outside. The `LedgerIntegrityBanner`
shipping a false all-clear on first paint is exactly that class of bug, and it
reached a device.

> A harness that cannot test a category of code should fail loudly when asked
> to, not silently return an empty object. There is no way to make Jest do
> that — so the compensating control is that the first test in a new category
> is treated as a config change, not just a test.

## Accepted risk — `totalPaid` is writable without a ledger entry

Recorded 2026-08-01, during review of Stage 2.

**The gap.** Stage 2's premise is that every naira in `totalPaid` traces to a
payment entry. The application enforces that — `recordPayment`, `createBatch`
and `markBatchesPaid` are the only writers and all three write an entry in the
same atomic update. **The database rules do not.**

```json
"totalPaid": { ".write": "… admin || staff" }
```

Any authenticated staff account can set `totalPaid` to any number with no
matching entry.

**Why it is not fixed.** `ServerValue.increment` writes to that exact path, and
RTDB rules cannot distinguish an increment from an arbitrary assignment — the
rule sees only the resulting value. Locking the path down would break every
legitimate payment. A rule of the form `newData.val() > data.val()` would allow
increases but still not tie them to an entry, and would block reversals.

Closing it properly means moving the balance out of the client's reach —
a Cloud Function or a server-side transaction that owns `totalPaid` and rejects
writes that do not accompany an entry. That is a Blaze-plan dependency and a
deployment surface this project does not otherwise have.

**Mitigation, which already exists:**

| Control | Where |
|---|---|
| The ledger is authoritative — `attachPayments` treats the sum of entries as truth and flags any disagreement | `payment-reconciliation.ts` |
| A mismatch is explained in words, not a red number, with a **Recalculate** action that resets the cache from the entries | `describeMismatch`, transaction detail |
| Drift is surfaced on the **dashboard**, not only on the cash page | `ledger-integrity-banner.tsx` |
| Entries themselves are create-only and immutable, so the audit trail cannot be edited to match a forged total | `database.rules.json` |

The last row is what makes this tolerable: a forged `totalPaid` cannot be made
*consistent*. It shows up as a mismatch the next time anyone opens the app,
against a ledger that cannot be rewritten to agree with it.

> A check you have to remember to open is a check that does not run. That is
> why the banner sits on the dashboard, renders nothing when the books agree,
> and names the largest gap when they do not.

## Dataset size — revisit Stage 4's priority

Measured 2026-08-01 against production, read-only.

The whole sales ledger is **~22 batches**: 14 already in the canonical layout
plus 6 more from the legacy migration (fewer once test data is removed). That is

| Scenario | Legacy records | Batches | Ledger |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| As found | 8 | 6 | ₦11,065,040 |
| Minus the `new` test record | 7 | 5 | ₦265,040 |
| **Decided — minus all four test records** | **4** | **2** | **₦187,460** |

All four (`new` ×1, `nw andn` ×3) were deleted as test data on 2026-08-01. The

**Two things follow.**

**1. Every dashboard figure looked at so far has been dominated by test noise.**
The four deleted records were 98.3% of the legacy ledger; the `new` record

**2. Stage 4's rollup work is far less urgent than the audit assumed.**
The audit argued for monthly and per-client rollups written on each sale, so

> **Revisit Stage 4's priority before building it.** Aggregates for a dataset
> this size are speculative optimisation. The cost of *not* having rollups is
> currently zero; the cost of having them is a second source of truth for money

>
> A better trigger than a stage number: revisit when the ledger passes a few
> thousand batches, or when a screen measurably drags on real data. Neither is
> close.

Note this does **not** downgrade the subscription-scoping work (`subscribeToRange`
reading month nodes). That bounds what is fetched over the network, which
matters on a phone regardless of dataset size.

# Part 2 — The original audit

*Reproduced verbatim from `Part 2 of this document`, 28 July 2026. Static review
only — no simulator, no live database, no production data volumes. Section
numbers are cited from source code and must not change.*

*Where a finding has since been addressed, Part 1 above is the current record —
this part is deliberately left as written so the original reasoning survives.*

## 0. Verdict up front

This is a genuinely well-organised codebase for a solo-built internal tool. The repository/normalized-type separation, the `STATUS_META` single source of truth, the pure `analytics.ts` selectors, and the honesty of the doc set (`PROJECT_STATUS.md` is better than most funded startups keep) all put it well above the usual "AI-assisted app" standard.

The problems are not layout or polish. They are three:

1. **The data layer will not scale past a few months of trading.** Every screen subscribes to the entire `sales` tree.
2. **The money model has no ledger and no concurrency safety.** Payments are a mutable float on the batch, updated by a client-side read-modify-write.
3. **Nothing is tested and the financial logic is where the bugs are.** Four concrete correctness bugs are identified below, all of which a handful of unit tests would have caught.

Everything else is secondary.

---

## 1. Architecture

### 1.1 What is working — keep these

| Decision | Why it's right |
|---|---|
| `services/sales-repository.ts` + `services/quote-repository.ts` as the only Firebase-path builders | Screens stay dumb. When you change storage layout (and you will — see 1.2), it's one file. |
| `Stored*` (raw) vs `SalesBatch`/`SalesRecord` (normalized) types | Correct instinct. The raw shape is Firebase's problem, not the UI's. |
| Status derived from amounts, never read from storage (`computePaymentStatus`) | Eliminates an entire class of "the chip says Paid but the balance is ₦40,000" bug. |
| `STATUS_META` as the only place status colours live | Genuinely enforced across most of the app. |
| `analytics.ts` as pure, I/O-free selectors | Best-designed file in the repo. Also the easiest to test — and currently untested. |
| `withAlpha()` instead of hex string-append | The reasoning documented in `PROJECT_STATUS.md` is exactly right. |
| Legacy read shim isolated to one function with a delete-me note | Textbook. Most codebases smear this everywhere. |
| Security rules are role-based and default-deny | The remediation from open rules landed properly. `users/$uid/role` self-escalation is blocked by the `.validate`. |
| `docs/` maintained as real handoff state | This is why an outside review is even possible. |

### 1.2 Critical — fix before the data grows

**(A) Whole-tree subscription. `sales-repository.ts:subscribeToBatches` → `db.ts:subscribe('sales')`**

```ts
export function subscribeToBatches(callback) {
  return dbService.subscribe(SALES_ROOT, (root) => callback(parseSalesTree(root)));
}
```

This downloads **every sale ever recorded** and re-walks the whole tree on **every single write**. `useRecords()` is consumed by Records, Clients, Board, Dashboard and Analytics — five surfaces, same firehose.

Cost projection at 20 sales/day with 3 line items each:

| Trading period | Batches | Approx. payload per sync |
|---|---|---|
| 1 month | ~500 | ~350 KB |
| 6 months | ~3,000 | ~2 MB |
| 2 years | ~12,000 | ~8 MB |

On Lagos mobile data, an 8 MB re-download every time your operator moves a kanban card is not a performance nit — it's an unusable app and a real airtime cost. And it happens on cold start of *every* screen.

The date-bucketed path `sales/{YYYY}/{MM}/{DD}/{receiptId}` was chosen to make this scoped, then the code ignores the buckets and reads the root anyway. Fix:

- `subscribeToRange(fromDate, toDate)` — subscribe to specific `sales/{YYYY}/{MM}` nodes, not `sales`.
- Records/Board/Dashboard default to current month; "All time" becomes an explicit, deliberate action.
- Clients and Analytics need cross-period totals — those should read from a **maintained rollup** (`aggregates/monthly/{YYYY-MM}` and `aggregates/clients/{clientId}`) written on each sale, not recomputed from raw history on the client.

Same pattern in `activity.ts:subscribeToActivity` — it pulls the entire activity log and then `.slice(0, limit)` client-side. Use `limitToLast(limit)`; the whole point of the `atMs` field is currently wasted. And `use-all-expenses.ts` subscribes to every month of expenses by design.

**(B) No payment ledger, and payments race.**

```ts
export async function recordPayment(batch: SalesBatch, amount: number) {
  const next = (batch.totalPaid || 0) + amount;   // read from stale client state
  await dbService.updateRecord(batch.dbPath, { totalPaid: next });
}
```

Two problems, both serious for a cash business with staff:

- **Lost writes.** Your admin records ₦5,000 on her phone while you record ₦10,000 on the desktop. Both read `totalPaid: 0`. Last write wins; ₦5,000 vanishes with no error and no trace. Needs `runTransaction()` or `increment()`.
- **No audit trail.** `totalPaid` is a single mutable number. You cannot answer: who collected this? when? cash or transfer? was it reversed? `logActivity` writes a *prose sentence* into an admin-only feed — that's a notification, not an accounting record, and it can't be reconciled or summed.

The fix is the single highest-value change in this document: **payments become append-only child records.**

```
sales/{YYYY}/{MM}/{DD}/{receiptId}/payments/{pushId}
  { amount, method, atMs, byUid, byName, note?, reversalOf? }
```

`totalPaid` becomes derived (sum of payments) or a transactionally-maintained cache. Corrections are new negative entries, never edits. That gives you a daily cash-reconciliation report for free — "Ada collected ₦47,500 today: ₦20,000 cash, ₦27,500 transfer" — which is the thing that actually protects you.

**(C) Money is a float.**

`totalAmount`, `unitPrice`, `total`, `totalPaid` are all plain JS numbers of Naira, and `formatCurrency` is:

```ts
return `₦${amount.toLocaleString()}`;
```

`toLocaleString()` with no options will happily render `₦1,234.5600000000001` once float error accumulates through `Math.max(subtotal, mov) + parseFloat(deliveryCost)`. Two fixes, pick one and be absolute about it:

- **Integer kobo** everywhere in storage, one formatter at the render edge. Correct, but a migration.
- **Minimum:** `Math.round()` at every write boundary and `toLocaleString('en-NG', { minimumFractionDigits: 0, maximumFractionDigits: 0 })` in the formatter.

Also add a `formatCurrencyCompact()` for dashboard KPIs — `₦2.4M` reads far better than `₦2,431,500` in a stat card.

**(D) Receipt IDs can silently overwrite.**

```ts
const rand = Math.random().toString(36).substring(2, 6).toUpperCase();  // 4 chars
...
await dbService.setRecord(dbPath, node);   // set() overwrites without complaint
```

Client-generated ID + `set()` = a collision destroys the earlier sale. The window is small (~1.7M combinations per day) but the failure is total and silent. Also: nothing stops two devices generating the same ID offline. Either use `push()` keys as the storage key (keeping the human-readable `receiptId` as a display field), or write via a transaction that fails when the node already exists. Add a `.validate` rule requiring `!data.exists()` for staff creates.

**(E) No submit guard on New Sale.** `submitBatch` has no `isSubmitting` state and the button isn't disabled during the `await`. On a slow connection your operator will tap twice and create two real sales with two different receipt IDs. This is a five-line fix and it is happening in production right now.

### 1.3 Correctness bugs found in review

**1. "Today" is computed in UTC. `analytics.ts:computeDashboardMetrics`**

```ts
const todayStr = now.toISOString().split('T')[0];
...
if (d.toISOString().split('T')[0] === todayStr) { todaySales += 1; ... }
```

Lagos is UTC+1. A sale logged at 00:30 WAT is `23:30` the *previous* day in UTC, so it drops out of "Today's Sales" on the dashboard. Meanwhile `createBatch` buckets the path using **local** date components, and `utils/date.ts:isToday` correctly uses local components — so you have three different definitions of "today" in one app. Standardise on local (or on an explicit `Africa/Lagos`) and delete every `toISOString().split('T')[0]`.

**2. `dueDate` is ignored by overdue logic.**

```ts
const status = computePaymentStatus(totalAmount, totalPaid, isOverdue(node.createdAt));
// isOverdue(createdAt, thresholdDays = 7)
```

A sale explicitly given a 30-day `dueDate` flags **Overdue** on day 8. A sale with a 3-day `dueDate` looks fine on day 5. The `dueDate` field is written, stored, editable in the UI, and then never consulted. Should be: `isOverdue(batch.dueDate ?? addDays(batch.createdAt, settings.defaultTermsDays))`.

**3. Minimum Order Value silently inflates the total.**

```ts
const finalBatchTotal = Math.max(batchSubtotal, settings?.mov || 1000) + delivery;
```

Your operator adds items totalling ₦600, and the app charges ₦1,000 with no line item, no label, and no explanation. The customer's invoice won't reconcile against the item list. MOV should appear as a visible adjustment row ("Minimum order adjustment: +₦400") in `BatchReviewCard` and on the invoice.

**4. Overdue and Unpaid are visually identical.** In `STATUS_META`, both are `color: '#ba1a1a', bg: '#ffdad6'`. The whole point of separating the statuses is that one demands a phone call today. Give Overdue its own treatment — deeper red, or a filled chip against Unpaid's outlined one, or a small clock glyph.

### 1.4 Structural issues

**Read-side RBAC is UI-only.** The rules grant `sales/.read` and `quotes/.read` to *any* authenticated user. `staffTodayOnly` in `use-records.ts` is a `.filter()` in JavaScript. Your machine operator can read every sale, every client, and every price you've ever charged straight from the Firebase SDK. With a 3-person team you may accept that — but decide it consciously rather than believing the UI is a boundary. If you want it real, the date-bucketed paths make it easy: `sales/$y/$m/$d/.read` conditioned on `$d === today || isAdmin`.

**No `.indexOn` in the rules.** `db.ts:subscribeQuery` uses `orderByChild`, which without a matching `.indexOn` makes Firebase download the node and sort on the client, with a console warning. Add `.indexOn` for any child you order by.

**Web/native fork duplication.** `index`/`records`/`clients`/`analytics`/`app-tabs`/`animated-icon` all exist as `.tsx` + `.web.tsx` pairs — roughly 1,700 LOC of parallel screens. Metro's platform-swap is the right *mechanism*, but the split is currently at the **screen** level, so business logic (filters, KPI math, bulk actions) is written twice and will drift. Push the split down: one screen per route owning the data and logic, delegating only *presentation* to `<RecordsView>` / `<RecordsView.web>`. `records.tsx` (190 LOC) and `records.web.tsx` (439 LOC) already differ in more than layout.

**Hard deletes contradict the audit-trail design.** `deleteBatch` → `remove()`. You log a `sale_deleted` activity message, but the sale itself is gone — including its payments. For a financial record, soft-delete (`voidedAt`, `voidedBy`, `voidReason`) with filtering on read, and let only admin void. Cancelled jobs are a normal event in printing; erasing them is not.

**No tests, no CI.** 14,382 LOC, real money, zero tests. `analytics.ts`, `payment-status.ts`, `date.ts` and `sales-repository.ts`'s normalizers are pure functions — every bug in §1.3 lives in them and every one is a five-line test. Add Jest + `jest-expo`, write ~30 tests over those four files, and a GitHub Action running `tsc --noEmit` + `expo lint` + `jest` on push. This is a two-hour job that pays for itself immediately.

### 1.5 Hygiene

- **121 hardcoded hex colours** across `src/app` and `src/components`, despite the theme + `STATUS_META` rule. That's the dark-mode bug class waiting to recur.
- **`Colors` in `constants/theme.ts` has only a `light` key**, and the file still carries the Expo template's boilerplate comment. Meanwhile `use-color-scheme.ts` and Paper's dark theme are both live. Either commit to dark mode or remove the scaffolding.
- **79 `any`s.** The worst is `batchItems: any[]` in `new-sales.tsx` — the *core* create path is untyped, which is why `JobDetailCard`'s output shape is unverified against `StoredItem`.
- **Firebase config hardcoded in `src/lib/firebase.ts`.** The web API key isn't a secret, but move it to `app.config.ts` + `EXPO_PUBLIC_*` env vars so staging/prod can differ, and restrict the key by referrer/package in Google Cloud Console.
- **`Alert.alert` vs bare `alert()`** mixed inside the same function in `new-sales.tsx`.
- **Error copy leaks internals:** `'Failed to submit batch. Check your connection or Firebase config.'` Your operator cannot check a Firebase config. Say what to do: "Couldn't save — you're offline. It'll send when you reconnect."
- **No error boundary** at the root layout. One render throw in a chart = white screen with no recovery.
- **Hardcoded `targetRevenue = 1000000`** in `use-records.ts` — belongs in Settings alongside `mov`.
- **Legacy shim + un-run migration** still outstanding per `PROJECT_STATUS.md`. Run it and delete `adaptLegacyRecords` — it currently double-walks the tree on every sync.

## 2. UI / UX

### 2.1 Strong

- **The dual-surface strategy is the right call.** Mobile as a lean field tool, web as the dense admin dashboard, one codebase. That's the correct read of how a print shop actually works — operator on the floor with a phone, owner at a desk.
- **The Records mobile card-list vs web data-table split** respects each form factor instead of shrinking a table.
- **Balance-led transaction detail** with collection-progress bar — leads with the number that matters (what's owed), not a receipt reprint.
- **⌘K command palette, density toggle, persisted filters, CSV export** — real admin-tool affordances, not decoration.
- **View-based charts with no chart library** — keeps the bundle lean and the visual language consistent. Correct trade-off at this scale.
- **Production kanban mapped to the actual 10ft-machine workflow** (Queued → Printing → Finishing → Ready → Delivered), and moving a card writes through to the database. The board is a real control surface, not a display.
- **Badge counts on the More menu** for ready-to-dispatch and clients-owing — pulls the two decisions that need attention up into the chrome.
- **Design tokens exist** (`Spacing`, `MaxContentWidth`, `WebContentMaxWidth`) and the web pages share one content column, so tab-switching doesn't shift edges.

### 2.2 Weak

**Accessibility is essentially absent.** 14 accessibility props across 10 files, against 72 components. Every icon-only button (`icon-button.tsx`, kanban controls, table sort headers) is unlabelled — invisible to a screen reader and, more practically for you, undiscoverable to a new staff member. Add `accessibilityLabel` + `accessibilityRole` to every icon-only control, and check that touch targets clear 44×44.

**No offline story, and this is your biggest UX gap.** The Firebase RTDB SDK queues writes offline, which means `await dbService.setRecord(...)` **never resolves** with no connection. Your operator taps "Submit", nothing happens, no spinner ends, no error appears. They tap again (see §1.2E). On Lagos connectivity this is the defining condition, not an edge case. You need:

- A persistent connection indicator (`.info/connected`) in the header.
- Optimistic UI: the sale appears in Records immediately, marked "Pending sync".
- Explicit copy on the button: "Saved locally — will sync when you're back online."

**Client name is free text.** No `clients` collection; `clients.web.tsx` aggregates by exact string match. `Blessing Prints`, `blessing prints`, and `Blessing Print` become three customers with three balances — and your debt-collection view quietly under-reports. This is the highest-value data-model addition: a real `clients/{clientId}` node, with typeahead on the New Sale form and merge-duplicates in admin. It also unlocks per-client history, credit limits, and WhatsApp reminders.

**No search on mobile Records beyond client name and material.** Your staff will search by receipt ID and by phone number — neither is matched in `useRecords`'s `searchedBatches` filter.

**Destructive actions.** `confirm-dialog.tsx` exists, but delete is a permanent erase of a financial record behind a single confirm. For voids, require typing the receipt ID, or at minimum a reason field (which you need for the audit trail anyway).

**Empty states are generic.** `empty-state.tsx` is reused everywhere. First-run empties should teach: the empty Board should say "Jobs appear here once you record a sale" with a link to New Sale, not "No items".

**No loading→content transition on the charts.** `loading-skeleton.tsx` exists but the analytics widgets appear to mount straight from empty, which reads as a broken page on slow data.

**Naira formatting in dense tables.** Full `₦1,234,500` strings in every cell of a paginated data table are hard to scan and compare. Right-align numeric columns, use tabular/monospace figures, and compact the KPI cards.

## 4. What to include next — features

Ordered by value to a Lagos large-format print shop, not by build difficulty.

### Tier 1 — directly makes or protects money

**Debtor follow-up with WhatsApp.** You already compute per-client outstanding balance. Add a "Owing" view sorted by age, one tap to open WhatsApp with a pre-filled, polite reminder including the receipt ID and balance. This is the single highest-ROI feature you could ship — it turns a report into collected cash, and it needs no new data model.

**Daily cash reconciliation.** Falls out of the payment ledger free: end-of-day total collected, split by method and by staff member, versus what's physically in the drawer. This is what closes the gap between trusting your staff and verifying.

**Material/roll inventory with consumption.** You already capture width, height, quantity and material per line item, so square-footage consumed is computable today. Deduct from roll stock, alert at reorder level. You explored the two-level model (Material Profiles for cashiers, Roll Log for admin) — that design is right and this is the largest remaining hole in the app. Without it, you can't tell whether a job was profitable or whether tomorrow's jobs are runnable.

**True job costing.** Revenue minus material consumed minus labour minus waste factor, per job. `settings` already holds `wasteFactor`, `laborCost`, `laminationCost`, `eyeletCost` — they're used for pricing but never for margin. Right now your dashboard shows gross revenue and calls the material-blind result "margin". A per-material and per-client profitability view would tell you which of your regulars you're subsidising.

**Deposit policy enforcement.** Configurable minimum advance (e.g. 50% before printing) with the Board refusing to move a job from Queued to Printing below threshold, overridable by admin with a reason. Turns a policy you enforce by memory into one the app enforces.

### Tier 2 — reduces friction and errors

**Reprint / repeat order.** "Same as last time" is the most common request in this business. One tap on a past job to clone it into a new sale.

**Quote expiry and follow-up.** Quotes have `Draft`/`Sent`/`Converted` but no expiry and no nudge. Material prices move; a 6-week-old quote at old prices is a loss. Add `validUntil`, an expiry badge, and a follow-up list for un-converted quotes.

**Delivery / dispatch tracking.** A `Delivered` stage exists on the Board but records no proof — who collected, when, signature or photo. Removes the "I never received it" dispute.

**Customer-facing job status link.** A read-only link (`/track/{receiptId}`) showing production stage and balance. Cuts the "is my banner ready?" calls significantly, and you already have the web surface to host it.

**Print-shop-shaped quote calculator.** Your sticker-layout and ink-coverage work already lives outside this app. Folding roll-width nesting into the quote form — "this artwork at this size wastes 40% of a 4ft roll; at 3.5ft it wastes 5%" — is a genuine competitive edge and it's logic you've already written elsewhere.

**Receipt via WhatsApp, not just PDF share.** `expo-print` → `Sharing.shareAsync` works, but a direct WhatsApp deep link with the invoice attached matches how your customers actually want to receive it.

### Tier 3 — later

- **Expense categories tied to jobs** (ink, transport, casual labour) so cost of goods is real rather than a monthly lump.
- **Recurring / contract clients** with agreed rate cards, so pricing doesn't depend on who's at the counter.
- **Supplier / purchase orders** for roll restocking, closing the inventory loop.
- **Staff performance view** — sales logged, collection rate, jobs completed per operator.
- **Backup export** — scheduled JSON/CSV dump to Drive. You have exactly one copy of your entire business history in one Firebase project with no export routine. Fix this earlier than "later" if nothing else on this list gets done.
- **Push notifications** for ready-to-dispatch and overdue balances.

## 5. The one-paragraph summary

The bones are good — repositories, typed domain model, derived status, pure selectors, honest docs. Three things need to change before this becomes the system of record for a real business: subscriptions must be scoped to date ranges instead of pulling the whole `sales` tree, payments must become an append-only ledger written transactionally instead of a mutable float, and the pure financial functions need tests (which will immediately catch the UTC "today" bug, the ignored `dueDate`, and the silent MOV inflation). After that, the two features that will change how the business runs are debtor follow-up over WhatsApp and material inventory with consumption tracking — both of which are mostly derivable from data you're already capturing.
